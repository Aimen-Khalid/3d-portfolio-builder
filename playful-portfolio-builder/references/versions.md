# The four reference builds

Read this **after** the person has picked a world (Step 3, before the concept sheet) or when you are stuck on how to make something feel physical. Do **not** read it while proposing worlds in Step 2: knowing these builds too early pulls every proposal toward a desk, a room or a cupboard.

All four were built for one person (Aimen Khalid, an engineering manager in Lahore) as her own portfolio. They show the quality bar and the techniques. **Their identities belong to her: never reskin them for anyone else**, even someone with a similar job or city. Learn the lesson, not the look.

Sharing a category or a technique is fine and expected (a lit surface seen from above, light through a window, objects that lift and turn over, a game tracker). What isn't fine is reusing a build's specific objects, their arrangement, or its wow moment with new labels on top. The test: with the person's name removed, could the site still only belong to them?

## The Desk: the main bar

A top-down desk under one low sun falling through a carved lattice screen. The techniques in `engine-lighting.md`, `html-in-canvas.md`, `material-effects.md`, `page-curl.md`, `lift-and-flip.md`, `sound-kit.md` and `game-tracker.md` all come from it.

**What it teaches**
- **A world that is personal, not generic.** Every object is real content: an ID badge, a notebook holding her journey, sticky notes for awards, a sealed letter, case files for projects, a card of kind words, a medal, a phone with her film, a visitor badge you fill in, and a basket that holds the résumé.
- **Real light that means something.** Height-mapped objects, raymarched soft shadows, AO, specular, steam, dust motes, and a light patch through a lattice. The presets are the five daily prayer times, which are part of her day; the light opens on the visitor's clock. Find the equivalent personal, cultural or place-based light for each person.
- **HTML-in-Canvas content that behaves like its material.** The notebook, letter, case files, kind-words card and visitor badge are live HTML drawn into the scene. Paper crumples under the cursor; only the tea's surface ripples.
- **Depth.** Objects rise toward you, settle at the centre and turn over to backs that look like real backs. The envelope comes forward, the seal cracks, the flap opens and the letter slides out.
- **A real page curl**, lit and shadowed.
- **Sound for everything**, all synthesised.
- **The payoff gathers the world.** The scene shakes, every object arcs into a container, it wobbles, the résumé rises out with Download and Put it back, and Put it back sends everything home.
- **The game:** 10 things to find, 3 secrets (type the name / tap every flower on touch, knock five times for a time-lapse through the day, clink the glass three times), and a polaroid reward.

**Her identity, not to be reskinned:** the walnut desk with block-printed runner, chai glass and jasmine garland, her carved lattice, the picnic-basket payoff, the prayer-time presets, and her objects and their arrangement. (Light through a window or screen, a basket or box payoff built around someone else's story, or time-based light presets that mean something to another person are all techniques, and fine.)

## The cards page: scrolling page with objects

A normal scrolling site with a nav (Journey, Kind words, Projects, Contact) where every section carries its own 3D object. The hero is a rail of lanyard badges, one per milestone, that you brush through and pick off the rail. Below it: a stack of kind-words cards you flip through with filters by company, a drawer of case files that slide out onto a desk and open, and a visitor badge the visitor prints with their own name. The close gathers it all and hands over the résumé.

**What it teaches**
- **A scrolling page can be a world.** The page reads and scans like a normal site (real headings, nav, text), yet every section is something to touch. The hero object gets the most effort and carries the first five seconds.
- **A different object and mechanic per section,** each made for its content: swinging badges for milestones, a card stack for quotes, a drawer for projects, a printer for the visitor.
- **Physics as delight.** Things swing when brushed, collide and settle; every touch plays a note, so idle play becomes a melody.
- **A collectible that lifts and flips**, carrying the detail on its own back.
- **A visitor object you personalise** (your own badge), which makes the visitor part of the world.
- **A payoff that packs everything into one container** that shakes and pops out the résumé.

**Her identity, not to be reskinned:** lanyard badges on a rail, the kalimba melody, her event badges, the kind-words card stack, the case-file drawer, the visitor badge and their order on the page. (A scrolling page with a hero object and a different object per section is a category, and fine for anyone.)

## Cozy room: walk-in space

An orbitable 3D room where each piece of furniture is a section: a desk with a laptop for projects, a wardrobe for experience, a trophy shelf for awards, a corkboard for the journey, a record player for kind words, a floor lamp.

**What it teaches**
- **Sections as furniture** you walk up to; the camera flies to each piece and back.
- **Small 3D props carry personality** (a DNA model, a stack of records, a sleeping cat).
- **A payoff that shakes the whole room** and then puts it back.

**Her identity, not to be reskinned:** her isometric room with its corkboard, record player, wardrobe and trophy shelf.

## Cupboard: walk-in space

A chest of drawers you open, with a companion cat.

**What it teaches**
- **Sections as physical containers you open** (drawers slide out, folders slide out of drawers).
- **A companion with personality** that watches the cursor and causes mischief the visitor fixes.
- **A lamp that relights everything** (night mode where brass catches the light), and **tilt** from the mouse or the phone's motion.

**Her identity, not to be reskinned:** her chest of drawers and the cat with its tricks.

## The common thread

None of these is good because of its objects. They are good because every object is real content, every reaction is what the material would do, the light changes and means something, there is something to find, and one moment people want to screen-record. Bring those qualities to a world that could only belong to the person in front of you.
