Blunt version: this paragraph could be on 10,000 other portfolios. Swap "Sana" for any other name and nothing changes. A recruiter will read it in two seconds, learn nothing, and keep scrolling.

**Line by line**

- **"Hi! I'm Sana."** Fine. It's the only line that's actually yours.
- **"passionate full-stack developer who loves building things"**: "Passionate" and "loves building things" are filler. Every developer says them, so they tell the reader nothing. "Full-stack" is the only useful word here, and it's still vague. Which stack?
- **"I have experience in many technologies"**: This is the weakest line. "Many" reads as "I'd rather not say." Name them. If the list is long, name the 3–4 you'd want to be hired for.
- **"I'm always learning"**: Everyone says this. It can also sound like "I'm still junior." Cut it, or show it with something specific you picked up recently and shipped.
- **"I'm a team player and a quick learner"**: These are résumé clichés, and they're claims with no proof. Nobody believes a soft skill you assign to yourself. Show it instead: a team you were on, a deadline you hit, something you learned in a week.
- **"Let's build something amazing together!"**: It's a generic sign-off that doesn't ask for anything. Build what? With whom? A call to action should say what you want: a job, freelance work, collaborators.

**The core problem:** every sentence is a claim about your personality. None of them is a fact about your work. The About section needs specifics: what you build, with what, for whom, and one result that proves it.

**Also:** five exclamation-mark-level sentences with no numbers, project names, or employers make it read junior, whatever your actual level is.
